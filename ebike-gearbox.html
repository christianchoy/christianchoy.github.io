01

The Challenge

Design and manufacture a compact transmission system for an electric bicycle.

Working in a team of five, the project involved developing an E-bike gearbox from initial concept through detailed design, manufacture, assembly and physical testing.

The transmission needed to satisfy mechanical performance requirements while also meeting strict manufacturing and material constraints.

03

Concept Development

Developing a transmission architecture within tight physical constraints.

Different arrangements of gears, shafts, bearings and transmission components were considered before the architecture was developed into a manufacturable design.

Packaging was particularly important because the gearbox needed to remain compact while maintaining suitable gear engagement and allowing the components to be manufactured from the available material.

Gear Layout & Packaging

Repositioning components helped create a more compact gearbox.

The position of the gears and shafts was refined throughout the design process to reduce the overall packaging volume while maintaining the required transmission arrangement.

One design decision I contributed to was repositioning elements of the gear train to achieve a more compact overall layout.

05

CAD Development

Converting the transmission concept into a complete mechanical assembly.

The gearbox was developed in CAD to define the positions and interfaces between gears, shafts, bearings, structural components and fasteners.

The digital assembly allowed interference, packaging and component relationships to be assessed before committing the design to manufacture.

Final gearbox CAD assembly

CAD assembly of the final E-bike gearbox design.

06

Design for Manufacture

The design had to work within the limitations of sheet acrylic manufacturing.

Components were manufactured from acrylic sheet, meaning that conventional solid gears and structural parts could not simply be manufactured as single components.

Some gears therefore required multiple acrylic layers, with larger gears constructed from four sheets of material. Smaller gears could be nested within larger components to reduce the total quantity of sheet material required.

Engineering Analysis

Structural analysis formed part of the project stage-gate process, helping to determine whether components satisfied the required performance criteria before the design could progress.

08

Final Design

A compact transmission developed for manufacture and physical testing.

The final gearbox brought together the gear train, supporting structure, shafts, bearings and fastening strategy within an assembly approximately 200 mm long, 60 mm wide and 185 mm high.

Clean final CAD render
09

Manufacture

Moving from CAD to real components exposed practical manufacturing challenges.

Once the design passed the required project stages, the gearbox components were manufactured from acrylic and assembled into the physical transmission.

The manufacturing stage highlighted the importance of tolerances, material behaviour and assembly planning, particularly where several layered components had to interact accurately.

Manufactured components
Gearbox during assembly
10

Assembly & Problem Solving

Manufacturing tolerances meant the CAD-perfect assembly required adjustment in practice.

During assembly, differences between the nominal CAD geometry and the manufactured acrylic components created fit and alignment challenges.

Components were carefully adjusted where required, including filing areas to improve fit and using acrylic adhesive to create the required layered assemblies.

This demonstrated how manufacturing tolerances and material behaviour can affect the performance of a mechanism even when the digital geometry is correct.

11

Physical Testing

The completed gearbox was tested as a functioning mechanical system.

Following assembly, the transmission was physically tested to evaluate its ability to transfer motion through the completed gear train.

The gearbox achieved 97 revolutions during a 30-second test period.

My Contribution

Contributing across design development, manufacture and team coordination.

My role included contributing to design decisions, manufacturing and assembly work while also maintaining meeting records throughout the project.

During design development, I contributed suggestions to improve the gearbox packaging, including repositioning elements of the gear arrangement to create a more compact design.

I also became increasingly involved in the practical technical work during the project, particularly during manufacture and assembly.

14

Reflection

The project changed how I think about the relationship between CAD and manufacture.

One of the biggest lessons from the project was that a component fitting perfectly within a CAD assembly does not guarantee that the manufactured system will assemble or operate in exactly the same way.

Manufacturing tolerances, adhesive joints and the behaviour of acrylic all affected the real assembly and required practical adjustments during the build.

The project also increased my confidence in contributing to technical design decisions. I became more willing to engage with CAD, manufacturing and mechanical problem solving rather than avoiding unfamiliar technical tasks.

If I were developing the gearbox again, I would place greater emphasis on tolerance analysis and design-for- assembly earlier in the CAD process, reducing the amount of adjustment required during manufacture.
