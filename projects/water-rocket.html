Project 01

Water Rocket Challenge

Simulation / Aerodynamics / Testing

University of Sheffield

2024

Result

1st place

among 245 Mechanical Engineering students

01

The Challenge

Design a water-powered rocket system capable of accurately delivering a payload.

The project required our team to design, analyse, manufacture and test a water rocket capable of reaching a target between 50 and 90 metres while clearing a 3 metre obstacle.

02

Design Requirements

The design had to balance range, accuracy, stability and rapid deployment.

Target Distance 50–90 m
Obstacle Height 3 m
Landing Zone 5 m
Launches 3
Time Limit 5 min
03

Concept Development

Developing a stable and repeatable launch system.

The team explored different approaches to the rocket geometry and launcher design, considering factors including aerodynamic stability, manufacturability, launch angle adjustment and repeatability.

Concept sketches / early designs
04

Simulation

Predicting the rocket trajectory before physical testing.

MATLAB was used within the project to model the rocket's flight and investigate how parameters such as launch conditions, thrust and aerodynamic drag affected the predicted trajectory.

MATLAB trajectory graph

Predicted rocket trajectory generated during the design process.

05

Aerodynamic Analysis

Understanding the aerodynamic behaviour of the rocket.

Computational fluid dynamics was used during development to investigate airflow around the rocket and assess the aerodynamic performance of the design.

CFD result

CFD visualisation used during aerodynamic analysis.

06

Structural Analysis

Checking that the launch system could withstand the expected loading.

Finite element analysis was used as part of the design process to assess critical components and verify the structural performance of the system before manufacture.

FEA result

Structural analysis undertaken during design development.

07

Final Design

Bringing the analysis and design decisions together.

Following concept development and engineering analysis, the final design combined the rocket and launch system into a solution that could be manufactured, adjusted and repeatedly deployed during testing and competition.

Final CAD render
08

Manufacture

Turning the digital design into a working system.

The final concept was manufactured and assembled before testing. The build process provided an opportunity to evaluate practical issues that were not necessarily apparent during the digital design stage.

Manufacturing photo 01
Manufacturing photo 02
09

Testing & Validation

Using physical testing to improve prediction accuracy and launch repeatability.

Physical launches were used to compare predicted and measured performance. Testing allowed the team to refine the relationship between the model and the real system before the final competition.

Rocket testing photograph

11

Reflection

What I learned from taking a design from prediction to physical testing.

The project demonstrated the importance of combining theoretical modelling with physical testing. Even a detailed simulation requires validation against the behaviour of the real system.

It also highlighted how design decisions across different areas of engineering — including aerodynamics, structural design, manufacture and testing — interact rather than existing independently.
